# Mission: PWM Signals in Automotive Electronics

## Why
To master Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) in automotive systems, enabling the user to diagnose and repair electrical components (e.g., radiator fans, fuel pumps, blower motors), understand ECU-controlled actuators, and design custom automotive electronics using hobby microcontrollers.

## Success looks like
- Explain the difference between PWM control and analog/resistive control, detailing the historical shift in automotive engineering.
- Measure and diagnose PWM signals (duty cycle, frequency, voltage levels) on a vehicle using a multimeter or oscilloscope.
- Design, program, and build a functional PWM-based motor/fan speed controller using an Arduino/ESP32.
- Read automotive wiring schematics and identify PWM control lines, driver circuits, and actuators.

## Constraints
- Self-paced learning.
- Intermediate electronic background (leverage existing understanding of voltage, current, and transistors).
- Needs clear practical tie-ins to real car components.

## Out of scope
- Deep dive into CAN/LIN bus protocols (touch upon them only where they interface with PWM controllers).
- Full ECU remap/tuning software theory (focus on hardware-level control signals instead).
